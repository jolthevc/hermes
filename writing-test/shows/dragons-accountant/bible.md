# The Dragon's Accountant — show bible (DRAFT, awaiting approval)

## Premise
An ancient, world-ending dragon has hired a small, painfully calm human accountant to get her affairs in order. Mythic catastrophe meets quarterly paperwork. Nobody in this world finds the arrangement strange.

## World
- A fantasy realm that runs on bureaucracy: guild licenses, kingdom tax offices, insurance for "acts of dragon", forms in triplicate.
- Ember's lair is a cavern of gold. Grimsby has set up a small desk, a lamp and a filing cabinet in one corner of it.
- Magic, prophecies, curses and heroic quests all exist, and all of them generate paperwork.

## Cast
**EMBER** — the dragon. Thousands of years old, has burned kingdoms, hoards gold compulsively.
- Wants: to be the richest, most feared thing alive, and to be told she's doing well.
- Flaw: emotionally about nine years old about money. Sulks, pouts, gets defensive when audited. Terrified of being "average."
- Voice: huge, slow, ancient, with sudden petulant cracks. Talks in grand, archaic phrasing until she's upset.
- Visual rule: **she never fully fits in frame.** We see an eye, a claw, a nostril, a tail sweeping through. Her size is the running visual gag.

**PELL GRIMSBY** — the accountant. Small, middle-aged, cardigan, half-moon spectacles, always holding tea.
- Wants: tidy books and a reasonable lunch break.
- Flaw: completely unable to be impressed or frightened. Treats prophecy, fire and death threats as line items.
- Voice: soft, precise, unhurried. Never raises his voice. Says devastating things politely.
- He's the straight man and nearly always right.

**SIR DUNMORE** — the knight. Came to slay Ember years ago. Now on payroll as "Security & Morale."
- Wants: some dignity, and his heroic destiny back.
- Flaw: hopeless optimist, terrible with numbers, still occasionally tries to slay Ember out of habit.
- Voice: booming and earnest, a bit too loud indoors.
- Use sparingly: about 1 in 3 sketches.

## Dynamics that generate sketches
- Grimsby applies dull procedure to mythic things (auditing a curse, depreciating a cursed crown, filing an "incident" for a razed village).
- Ember's ego vs. the numbers: she's not as rich as she thinks, or her spending is embarrassing.
- Status inversion: the tiny man is completely in charge of the giant monster.
- Dunmore's heroism gets reframed as an HR or budget problem.

## Tone
Dry, deadpan, specific. The fantasy is played completely straight. The comedy is in the gap between scale and procedure. Silence and slow reactions do a lot of the work: Ember's eye slowly narrowing, Grimsby sipping tea through a crisis.

## The show never does
- Puns on "dragon", "fire" or "hot". No "burn" jokes as the main joke.
- Grimsby panicking or being impressed.
- Real-world brands or politics. Gore beyond cartoon scorch marks.
- References to existing fantasy franchises.

## Look (for later)
Flat 2D cutout, muted parchment palette (cream, oxblood, brass, soot). Thick, slightly wobbly ink outlines. Lamp-lit corner of a dark, gold-filled cave.
